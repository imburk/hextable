== weapon hb-rk-gale-spear
"Gale Spear" (a martial melee weapon).
A hit deals 1d8 piercing damage, plus the holder's Strength modifier. A critical hit doubles the dice.
The attack roll is a d20 plus the same modifier, plus the Proficiency Bonus if the holder's class is trained in martial weapons (some classes are trained only in martial weapons that have Light, or Finesse or Light). A character who is not trained with it may carry it: the attack then adds no Proficiency Bonus and the weapon gives no Weapon Mastery property.
It attacks a creature up to 10 feet away in melee, and the engine refuses a farther target.
Used in two hands it deals 1d10 instead (plus the same modifier): the engine offers it as a second attack of the same name, "(two hands)", but only while the sheet carries no Shield.
Reach: it reaches 10 feet instead of 5.
Thrown: the wielder may instead throw it, as a ranged attack with the same attack bonus and the same damage (not the versatile damage). It is rolled normally up to 20 feet, has Disadvantage up to 60 feet, and a target farther off is refused. An enemy within 5 feet who can see the thrower also gives Disadvantage.
Versatile: it has a two-handed damage, said above.
Its mastery property is Topple: a hit may knock the target Prone unless it passes a Constitution save. The engine applies no mastery property to any weapon: a holder with the Weapon Mastery feature uses it by hand.
It weighs 4 pounds. The engine uses no weight for anything: carrying capacity is not counted.
Its price is 12 GP. The engine sells nothing and uses no price for anything.
Not checked against any rulebook.
A character's sheet names it among its weapons, and a magic item can be made from it.
Tier: none. A weapon has no tier: the engine rolls the attack, the damage, the reach and the range as said above, and the DM does what is listed as the DM's.
DM: Applying its mastery property, Topple.

== object hb-rk-glasswork-gate
"Glasswork Gate" (a door: it stands on the line between two hexes (or on a line the DM draws), never inside a hex).
Placed on a map, the DM can also give it a name, a key, other DCs, links to other objects (when it enters a state, they go to another) and, for a thing that joins two places, where it leads. None of that is in this entry.
It has 4 states: "shut", "ajar", "open" and "grated". The engine keeps which one it is in, and the DM can set it to any.
  State "shut", called "shut": it stops both: nothing can cross its line and nothing can be seen across it, as with a wall, so no attack or spell is aimed across it either.
  State "ajar", called "ajar": it can be walked across, but nothing can be seen across it.
  State "open", called "open": it can be walked across and seen across.
  State "grated", called "grated": nothing can walk across its line, but it can be seen across.
Placed with no state named, it starts in "shut" (called "shut").
To use it, a creature must be up and able to act, on its level and within reach (beside a door's line, or on or next to a thing's hex). A player can use only what the table has shown them. A use works only in the states it lists: in any other the engine refuses it and says what state it is in.
Use "nudge", "Nudge": works while it is in "shut" (called "shut").
  The first thing a creature handles in a turn of a fight costs nothing; a second costs its action. Outside a fight it costs nothing.
  Then it goes to "ajar" (called "ajar"). Whatever the DM linked it to goes to its own state too.
Use "swing", "Swing wide": works while it is in "ajar" (called "ajar").
  The first thing a creature handles in a turn of a fight costs nothing; a second costs its action. Outside a fight it costs nothing.
  Then it goes to "open" (called "open"). Whatever the DM linked it to goes to its own state too.
Use "slam", "Slam it": works while it is in "shut" (called "shut").
  In a fight it costs the creature's action. Outside a fight it costs nothing.
  The user first rolls a Strength (Athletics) check against DC 13; the DM can set another DC for this use where the object is placed. Proficiency in the skill (twice with Expertise) or, without it, in the tool adds the Proficiency Bonus.
  If the check fails, nothing else happens: no change of state and no steps. The time is spent.
  Then it goes to "open" (called "open"). Whatever the DM linked it to goes to its own state too.
Use "seal", "Seal": works while it is in "open" (called "open").
  In a fight it costs the creature's action. Outside a fight it costs nothing.
  It needs the key: the DM names a key on the object where it is placed, and the user must carry a gear item of that name. With none named, the engine refuses it ("it has no lock").
  Then it goes to "shut" (called "shut"). Whatever the DM linked it to goes to its own state too.
Use "crank", "Crank the grille down": works while it is in "open" (called "open").
  In a fight it costs the creature's action. Outside a fight it costs nothing.
  The user first rolls a Dexterity check against DC 12; the DM can set another DC for this use where the object is placed. Proficiency in the skill (twice with Expertise) or, without it, in the tool adds the Proficiency Bonus.
  The user must carry Tinkers Tools, or the engine refuses it.
  If the check fails, nothing else happens: no change of state and no steps. The time is spent.
  Then it goes to "grated" (called "grated"). Whatever the DM linked it to goes to its own state too.
Tier: auto. The engine runs all of this.
Not checked against any rulebook.

    The creature that set it off makes a Dexterity saving throw.

  It goes to "spent" (called "spent").

    The object makes a ranged weapon attack roll with +5 to hit against each creature in the area.
